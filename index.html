THE CONSTRAINT

Every watt of compute becomes a watt of heat.

Air cooling ended when AI racks passed 100 kW. In vacuum there is no convection at all: heat leaves only by radiation, and radiator area dominates mass, launch cost, and system architecture. At the bottom of the stack, quantum processors need millikelvin temperatures and depend on a helium-3 supply that cannot scale.

IMMEDIATE MARKET DEMAND

One constraint. Four markets.

01

High-Density Data Centres

Racks above 100 kW require liquid, and liquid moves the bottleneck to rejection. Thermal management is a US$13.2B market in 2026, growing to US$32.4B by 2032.

02

Quantum Cryogenics

Dilution refrigerators depend on helium-3, sourced almost entirely from decaying tritium stockpiles. Supply runs 10,000 to 30,000 litres per year below demand.

03

Orbital Compute

Eight or more companies are racing to run GPUs in orbit. None has solved radiative rejection at megawatt scale.

04

Lunar Infrastructure

A 354-hour night, 300 °C thermal cycling, and abrasive regolith. Thermal control is the enabling subsystem for every surface operation.

CORE TECHNOLOGY

Three problems, one discipline.

01

High-Temperature Rejection

Radiated power scales with the fourth power of surface temperature. Raising rejection temperature is the only non-linear lever available. Every kelvin gained removes area, mass, and launch cost.
